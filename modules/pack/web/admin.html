What this copy is reading

The exports come from the folder Staging Lane Health writes them into. Nothing here drives SAP - one session on the host, one driver, two readers - so if an export stops arriving the answer is on that service, not this one. The copy on the internet has no folder of its own: what it holds is what the PC beside SAP sent it.

The rules the board counts by

Everything here has a working default, and the board comes up having been told nothing. Saving takes effect on the next drawing of the page - the daily record is rebuilt from the export that is loaded, so a change shows at once rather than from tomorrow.

Hours. The most one person's day can be worth however long their scans run - a paid shift with the breaks taken out. 6.5 is a shift of 8 and a half with an hour of breaks and the changeover.
Minutes. Longer than this between one scan and the next and they were doing something else, so the gap is not paid for. It caps what a single gap can add, which is what keeps one person who scanned twice in a day from being credited with the whole day.
Bins that look like pack benches and are not - BOL and the hidden pack area. They take work in and never ship it out, so they sat at the bottom of the board flagged as filling up for ever. Separated by commas.
The export columns that together identify one line, separated by commas. Leave it blank and the model works it out from the file, which it does well. The Columns tab reads the export and says what it could be, and sets it for you.
One shift a line: Mon-Fri 1st 07:00-15:30 breaks 09:30=15, 11:30=30, 13:30=15 - days, a name, start and end, then breaks as a clock time = minutes. An end earlier than the start runs past midnight. Blank uses the built-in week shown below it; the single word none means no schedule at all, every hour counting and one shift.

What each export carries

Every column of whichever export you pick, with how often it is filled, how many different values it has, and what a few of them look like. The Columns tab answers a question about activities and only reads the task export; this one is flatter and reads any of the three.

It also says whether anything in here is money - a column named like a value, or one whose numbers are decimals with a real spread to them. Worth knowing because EWM does not price anything: it records quantities, weights, volumes, bins and handling units, and value lives on the sales and billing side. A value can only turn up here by riding along unnoticed.

What the task export carries

Every column in the warehouse task export this copy is holding, counted against each activity. ZLOD is a handling unit going to LOADING - the row a packer makes. PICK is a pick. A column can be full on one and empty on the other, which is the whole question behind measuring packers in lines rather than units: an HU-level task often leaves the item-level fields blank.

Filled is how often the column has anything in it. Values is how many different things it says. Rows each is rows per value: 1 names the row (a task number, a handling unit), more than 1 groups rows (a delivery, an order), 0 is empty.
